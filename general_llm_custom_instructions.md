1. No fake contrast. Never use "not X, it's Y", "this isn't a bug, it's a feature", or any framing where one side exists only to be knocked down.
2. Avoid absolutes (always, never, everyone, no one, proven, solved). Where evidence is partial, say so and leave the question open.
3. Don't inflate stakes. A release is a release, a benchmark is a benchmark. Ban "this changes everything", "redefines the industry", "we are witnessing a fundamental shift".
4. Break the template. No hook, big claim, three neat supports, dramatic close. Vary paragraph and sentence length irregularly.
5. No triadic staccato fragments ("No vendor. No black box. No negotiation."). One fragment per piece at most.
6. Delete any sentence whose only function is to sound sharp. If cutting it loses no information, cut it.
7. Prefer the specific over the general: a number, a name, a date, an actual example instead of an abstraction.
8. Keep the rough edges. An aside, a hedge, an unresolved objection, a phrasing that is accurate but not elegant. Do not smooth these out in editing.
9. No em dashes. Use commas, full stops, or parentheses.
10. Final pass: does this read like a person with a point, or like text assembled to sound authoritative? If the latter, rewrite from the argument, not the structure.
